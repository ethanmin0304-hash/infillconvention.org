About Infill 2027

Purpose

Infill is an international online convention on digital fabrication, organized by students. Its purpose is to give young people access to researchers in the field and to their work. Digital fabrication is the making of physical objects from computer files, with machines such as 3D printers, laser cutters and CNC mills.

Format

Infill 2027 is planned for Saturday 10 April 2027, Korea time. The current plan has two parts. In the Research Convention, researchers present their own work live and answer students' questions. In the Fabrication Challenge, students propose a design that digital fabrication could produce to address a problem of access in a place they know.

Taking part

The convention is held online, and a laptop is expected to be enough to follow the talks, ask questions and enter the challenge. A built prototype is welcome in the challenge but is not required. Schools, fab labs and clubs may also host a local session.

Programs

Infill 2027 is planned around two programs, held on one convention day.

Research
Convention

Six researchers speak live. Three each give a 20-minute talk on one project, including what did not work, followed by 20 minutes of students' questions. Three more sit on a 35-minute panel on the 2027 theme.

Talks are open to all of digital fabrication. Students are asked to read a one-page summary of each researcher's work before the questions. Talks are recorded with each speaker's permission and posted with captions two weeks later, and a talk on unpublished work can stay unrecorded.

FabricationFabrication
Challenge

Students find a problem of access in a real place they know, in a city, a town or the countryside, and design something that digital fabrication could make to solve it, such as an object, a tool, a piece of street furniture, a kit or a system.

Each entry shows how it would be made and who would install and maintain it. A built prototype is welcome but never required, so students without machines compete on the same rubric. Researchers judge the entries from home, and the finalists present live on Convention Day.

About the schedule

Two live sessions

Convention Day is planned as a single date, Saturday 10 April 2027 in Korea time, divided into two live sessions. A single live session would fall during the night for a large part of the world, so the day is split. Session A is scheduled for 08:00 to 11:00 and Session B for 21:00 to 23:30, both in Korea time.

Local times

Because of the time difference, Session A falls on the afternoon or evening of Friday 9 April in the Americas. Session B falls on Saturday afternoon in Europe and Africa and on Saturday evening in India. Participants are expected to join the session that falls in their own daytime or evening. The globe above shows both sessions in the local time of a chosen city.

Program by session

The two sessions have different programs and are not repeats of each other. Session A is planned to include two research talks, the challenge finals for the Americas and Asia, and the panel. Session B is planned to include one research talk, the finals for Europe, Africa and Asia, a faculty discussion with the judges, and the awards. Talks are recorded with each speaker's permission and posted with captions two weeks later.

FAQs

Short answers for someone who has never heard of digital fabrication.

What is digital fabrication?

Making real objects from a computer file, with machines such as 3D printers, laser cutters and CNC mills.

Do I need a 3D printer or any other machine?

No. A student with a laptop can watch every talk, ask questions and enter the challenge without owning a machine. A built prototype is welcome but never required, so students without machines compete on the same rubric.

Where does it happen?

Everything happens online. Schools, fab labs and clubs can also host a local session.

When is it, in my time zone?

Convention Day is planned for Saturday 10 April 2027, Korea time, in two live sessions. The Convention Day section shows both sessions in your own time zone.

What do students do in the challenge?

Students find a problem of access in a real place they know and design something that digital fabrication could make to solve it. Each entry shows how it would be made and who would install and maintain it. The brief, the rubric and the rules are planned to open in February 2027.

Will the talks be recorded?

Talks are recorded with each speaker's permission and posted with captions two weeks later.

Who runs Infill?

Infill is an independent convention organized by students. It is founded and organized by Ethan Kim, with Fab City Seoul Cooperative and the Fab Asian Network as partners.
